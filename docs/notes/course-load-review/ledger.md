The three independent challenge notes are the detailed per-finding ledger: [early 00–08](sceptic-early.md), [middle 09–18](sceptic-middle.md), [late 19–25](sceptic-late.md). Read these alongside the original reader notes. Findings were not silently deleted or rewritten when challenged.

## Surviving directions

- Masks: move basic-rule experimentation before field/extraction material. Branches: defer the opening compiler source-attribution diversion. Both are concrete sequencing interruptions; neither establishes novice failure rates.
- Memory/stack: compact matched byte/extension/addressing/frame state where the current view forces reference travel. Preserve model-derived values, explicit addresses, changed/unchanged evidence and access to full detail.
- Product halves: distinguish completed comparison from an independently stepped current state. Downgraded to low: the DIAGRAM role and model note partially signal its independence.
- Boot/debugging: bring placement/startup support to first use and group debugger commands at semantic checkpoints. Preserve the native assembly capstone and familiar arithmetic. Treat trusting the supplied output routine on the first pass as a design candidate; the existing startup text is locally explained.
- Link the debugging task directly to the preceding lesson’s requested startup figure. Compiler-contract comparison and A32 record-reading have accepted reference-access findings: show the compared instructions and make the requested excerpt readily accessible.
- The strings lesson should expose its required `break` definition rather than leave that first definition in an optional disclosure. Other local reference/duplication issues remain lower priority; their exact severity and smallest direction are in the sceptic notes.

## Downgraded or rejected inferences

- **Module 0 is blocked by an untaught operand rule:** rejected as a medium prerequisite barrier. The first prediction is exploratory and any answer unlocks explanation. Explicit discovery framing is an optional low-level improvement.
- **The proposed operation-first sequence is proven best:** rejected. It is a prototype justified by the user's observed difficulty. Test a compact first operation and transfer before committing to a complete new curriculum.
- **Masks construction proves excessive novelty:** downgraded to a design hypothesis. Rules, field read, hints and attempt stepping exist. A staged in-range attempt is a worthwhile proposal, not a proven prerequisite repair.
- **Load widths require wholesale reordering:** not established; the first task and construction already isolate a signed-byte load, and familiar w/x clearing is legitimate reuse. Improve comparison presentation first.
- **Multiplication's instruction family proves overload:** rejected. Low-product/MADD construction is bounded. Grouping MSUB differently is optional coherence work.
- **Bit grids always make loops too difficult:** not established. Decimal-first is a presentation trial, not a universal defect finding.
- **Checked arithmetic lacks prior machine mechanisms:** rejected. Casts, extension, flags, stores and selection were taught. Moving formatting after checked-add is a focus proposal, not a prerequisite fix.
- **Predictions before worked explanations or taught rules before transfer questions are spoilers:** rejected as blanket claims. Inspect the actual gate, task and example instead.
- **Whole functions, large figure counts, or long pages demonstrate overload:** rejected. Later synthesis is purposeful; task-specific reference travel and genuinely new operations need separate evidence.

No fixes were implemented, so no correction-verification claims or deployment claims follow from these decisions. A future implementation should obtain independent checks of affected built states under the repository's review rules.
